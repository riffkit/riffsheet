# Changelog

The tool's version is printed by `python3 -I scripts/riffsheet.py --version`, with the version of the `riffsheet.json` format it writes (`format_version`).

## 0.2.0

The analysis says what this machine could and could not do for it, never drops a stage silently, and marks what came from outside the tool. The tool reports facts and measures; the agent judges what an analysis needs and how to get it; the person agrees to anything that costs them something.

- New `check [VIDEO] [--json]`: facts only, no network, nothing changed. ffmpeg's version and any filter the tool needs that it lacks; the tool's own speech recognition path (`transcribe`, faster-whisper with the `small` model) as ready, missing or partly there (package installed but model not here, or installed only in `~/.riffsheet/venv`), with what it adds, how long it runs, what preparing it costs (the package about 64 MB to download, 207 MB on disk and about 5 s to install, measured for 1.2.1 on Python 3.9 on macOS; the model 486 MB; both over the network; no audio or video sent anywhere), the commands that prepare it in a virtual environment of its own, leaving the system Python as it is, the commands that remove what preparing adds, and other ways to get a transcript. With a video it states plain exclusions (no audio track: nothing to transcribe). It ends by saying what it covers and that it knows nothing about other tools on the machine. It does not judge what a video needs.
- Every optional stage ends in one of three recorded states: ran (by what, and whether that was the tool's tested path), failed (it was there and stopped while running, and why) or did not run (why). `scan` writes them to `index.json` (`stages`), `build` to `riffsheet.json` (`_local.stages`), and when one failed or did not run, `riffsheet.md` says so under its first line. When `transcribe` cannot make a transcript it exits 1 as before and now also leaves at `--out` a record of why, which `scan --transcript` takes; an error inside the recognizer is reported in one line instead of a traceback.
- Which speech recognizer to use is the agent's choice. `scan` needs a word-timed transcript in the documented format; `transcribe` is the one tested path, with its measurements in `references/TOOL.md`. A transcript from another recognizer, another model size or the person is recorded with what made it (`scan --transcript-by`), and the riffsheet says plainly that it was not the tested path.
- Means outside the tool: facts the agent establishes by its own means go in the judgment's `own_means` with what it used, and gaps it could not fill in `tool_gaps`; `riffsheet.md` shows both in sections of their own, and `riffsheet.json` carries `_local.own_means`.
- A video analyzed without speech recognition no longer reads as "no speech": the line under the title says `speech not transcribed`, `metadata.speaking_mode` is `none` tagged `cannot_judge_locally`, the scan summary and the build sheet say why there are no words and do not set loudness events against words, and a sentence in the prose or report saying that nobody speaks is a problem `build` lists.
- The instructions: the agent judges from the request and the pictures whether a capability matters, and does so whenever a need appears; it runs `check`, asks once with what it adds and every cost (download, disk, time, network, and whether anything would send the person's audio or video to an outside service), prepares nothing without a yes, and otherwise carries on and says what the result lacks. It reports to the person in their language in its reply (one plain line per shot, why the video holds a viewer, what their version has to keep), and writes a full translation of `riffsheet.md` (`riffsheet.zh.md` and so on, every time, number, spoken line and on-screen word unchanged) only when asked; the checked files stay in English.
- An agent that can hear may listen. Whether it can is settled by a test the tool sets and marks, not by its say-so: `hear-check DIR` writes a short sound file in which the machine's speech synthesizer (`say`, `espeak-ng` or `espeak`) says six random digits, kept on disk only as a slow salted hash; the agent reports what it heard (`--answer`) or that it cannot open sound (`--cannot-hear`), and the tool records passed or failed. After a pass, `listen DIR` cuts the sound around each measured sound event, and the whole track of a video up to 60 s, into files to open, and `build` takes the judgment's `heard` statements (music and its kind, tone of voice, what a sound effect is), each marked heard by the agent, into `audio.heard` and each beat of `riffsheet.md`; without a pass it refuses them. `riffsheet.md` says near its top whether the sound was heard or not judged, and why (`_local.hearing`). `check` says whether the hearing check can be made on this machine. Verified with a hearing model driven by a script, not yet with a full coding agent that can hear.
- A third issue template: the tool lacked something.
- `format_version` stays 1: everything added to `riffsheet.json` is optional (`_local.stages`, `_local.own_means`, `_local.hearing` and `audio.heard` are absent in files written before), and no field changed type or meaning; `_src.speaking_mode` uses a value the format already had, and `_src` gains the value `heard`.
- The two examples are rebuilt so that their `riffsheet.json` has `_local.stages`; the agents' words are unchanged.

## 0.1.0

First public release.

- `scan` measures a short video once: cuts on true frame timestamps, including a jump cut inside one framing when a tenth of the picture or more changes in one frame and keeps its colors; other changes, shot stillness, text-like regions, fixed layers, split layouts, drawn elements, and the sound (levels, silences, cliffs and peaks, what lies under the voice). It says where loudness peaks, rises and drops fall on the cuts, against how many would by chance (SOUND AT THE CUTS). It lays out the overview, one frame per shot, change events and possible jump cuts within a vision model's image budget.
- Follow-up commands for what an agent needs to look at: `text`, `element`, `zoom`, `frames`, `mouth` and `color`; the optional `transcribe` makes a word-timed transcript with faster-whisper.
- `build` prints the measured sheet a judgment is written against, and from the agent's judgment file writes `riffsheet.json` (format_version 1, `schema/riffsheet.schema.json`) and `riffsheet.md`, after checking the judgment against the measurements; statements about details must each name a view that shows them. It also says where sound events fall on the caption appearances, and puts both sound measures in `_local.sound_at_picture` and in one line of `riffsheet.md`.
- Exit status: 0 done, 1 an error, 2 a usage error, 3 a refused `build`.
- Python 3.9 or newer, standard library only; ffmpeg 7 or newer.
- Two examples, each a first run by an agent new to the project, with its commands, judgment file and pictures: `examples/red-carpet` (12 s, music, no speech) and `examples/outfit-rules` (8 s, Spanish to camera).
- A plugin marketplace file for Claude Code (`.claude-plugin/marketplace.json`).
